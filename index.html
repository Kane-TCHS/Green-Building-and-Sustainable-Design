Part 1 – What does "green" mean?

Sustainability means meeting our needs today without making it harder for future generations to meet theirs. One way to put it: we don't own the earth; we hold it in trust for our children.

Green building applies that idea to buildings, all the way through planning, design, construction, daily use and eventually demolition. Good green design balances three things, often called the Triple Bottom Line:

  • People: is it healthy and comfortable to live in?
  • Planet: does it use less energy, water and materials?
  • Profit: does it make financial sense to build and run?

For an affordable home, all three count. A house that's green but too expensive to build doesn't help your family, and neither does one that's cheap to build but has a $400 power bill every month.

Part 2 – Six ways to build green

These six goals come from the Whole Building Design Guide, which architects and engineers use. The drawing below shows how many of them can come together in one small Florida house.

1Use the site well

Work with the land instead of against it. Keep existing trees, especially big shade trees on the south and west, disturb as little soil as possible, and face the house to catch breezes and avoid harsh afternoon sun.

Florida example: a large oak on the west side can shade the house all afternoon.

2Save energy

In Florida, saving energy mostly means keeping heat out. Roof overhangs, a light-colored "cool roof," good attic insulation, windows that block solar heat, and an efficient heat-pump AC all help. Solar panels can make some of the house's own electricity.

Overhangs work best on the south side. The summer sun is almost straight overhead at midday, so even a short overhang blocks it:

Florida example: a 2-foot roof overhang shades south walls and windows from the high summer sun.

3Save water

Use less water inside and outside. Low-flow toilets and showerheads (look for the WaterSense label) save water every day. Outdoors, collect rainwater and plant things that don't need sprinklers.

Florida example: Florida gets plenty of rain in summer but has dry spells in spring, so rain barrels help bridge the gap.

4Smart space and materials

A smaller, well-planned house uses fewer materials and costs less to cool. Pick materials that are durable, local or made from recycled content, and plan room sizes around standard material sizes (like 4′ × 8′ sheets) to cut waste.

Florida example: materials that hold up to humidity and hurricanes don't need replacing as often.

5Healthy indoor air

People spend most of their time indoors, so the air inside matters. Choose low-VOC paints and finishes (VOCs are chemicals that off-gas into the air), vent bathrooms and kitchens to the outside, and control humidity so mold can't grow.

Florida example: high humidity makes mold a real risk, so bathroom exhaust fans and good AC sizing are important.

6Easy upkeep

A green house should stay green without much effort. Simple systems, durable finishes and easy-to-reach filters make that more likely.

Florida example: an AC filter that's easy to reach is more likely to get changed on schedule.

Part 3 – A building's life cycle

Green thinking covers a building's whole life:

  • Pre-Building: choosing the site, designing and making materials, before anything is installed.
  • Building: construction and the years people live in the house.
  • Post-Building: remodeling or demolition, when old materials get reused, recycled or thrown away.

Construction and demolition produce a lot of waste. According to the EPA, the U.S. generates more than twice as much construction and demolition debris as regular household trash. That's why planning ahead and recycling on site matter.

Part 4 – Green Features Plan (16 pts)

Now apply what you learned to your house. For each of the 8 items, write:

  • What: the specific feature or strategy
  • Why: why it fits your Florida house and your client family

Aim for 2–3 sentences per item. Use your Client Survey and Module 1 work.

❌ Weak"Solar panels because they're green."
✅ Strong"We'll add 2-foot overhangs on the south side and plant a shade tree on the west side. Our family said the house can't be too hot because both parents work outside all day, so shading the house will keep it cooler and lower their AC bill."
A strong answer names a specific feature, says where it goes, and ties it to the family or to Florida.
